"What I love about working at Arch Rock is the family that has been created. We all work together from the morning cleaning dude to the closing dishwasher." — Kami
"I can confidently say that I have experienced the most teamwork and general cohesiveness among staff members since working here." — Ruby

Compete for staff, not customers

Jon Myerow of Tria Cafe in Philadelphia runs one of the most selective restaurants in the country — and he's clear about why.

"To me the biggest competition is not for customers. It's for staff. If you compete in the labor market and get the best staff, the customers will follow."

Customers gravitate to businesses they feel good about

Dave Query of the Big Red F Restaurant Group put it simply:

"Customers gravitate towards businesses they feel good about supporting. You make your back alley look as good as your front door. Every effort you make to impress upon your customers what you're trying to do makes a big impression — because they're watching."

The simplest rule

After a trip to the vet where no one thanked him for a $205 bill, Wes boiled it down:

"You just have to thank people for doing business with you. Today, more than ever, a good product is not enough. Customers need to feel like you appreciate that they spend their hard-earned money with you."
