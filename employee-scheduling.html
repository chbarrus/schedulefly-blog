The real problem: it was never a scheduling problem

When Wes started coding Schedulefly, he was drawing on a very specific memory — building the schedule for 40 wait staff. And here's what he realized: the hard part was never the schedule itself. It was communication.

"Phone calls, paper requests, voice mails, messages left at the host stand on sticky notes, and verbal requests exchanged over beers the night before the schedule was made were the ways we communicated. Looking back it was a gigantic mess."

Every restaurant owner and manager knows this feeling. You finally post the schedule, and within minutes the changes start. Shift swaps. Time-off requests. "I didn't know I was working." By the end of the week, the paper schedule in the back office is illegible with eraser marks and sticky notes.

Schedulefly exists to make that entire loop — building, posting, changing, and communicating the schedule — fast, clean, and painless.

How Schedulefly scheduling works

Managers create the weekly schedule online, from anywhere. Staff see it instantly — on their phone, by email, or by text message. When something changes, everyone who needs to know finds out immediately, with no phone calls.

The whole thing replaces the paper book (or the Excel spreadsheet) with one simple web app that both managers and staff actually enjoy using.

Why we'll never auto-schedule

Some scheduling software promises to generate the schedule automatically based on availability, seniority, and sales. We've deliberately chosen not to do that. Here's why:

"I can't imagine something like that being anywhere near useful unless you were scheduling machines — or robots. People are really difficult to schedule, and creating a winning team of the right crew night after night often has more to do with the people and their personalities and less to do with their skill level."

Scheduling a restaurant is an art, not a science. Our job is to make the execution — and all the changes that come after — easier, faster, and a little more fun.
