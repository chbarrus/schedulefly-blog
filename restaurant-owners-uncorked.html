What Restaurant Owners Uncorked is

Restaurant Owners Uncorked began as a simple idea: tell the stories of the independent restaurant owners who use Schedulefly. Those stories became a book, then a video series, then a podcast, then a second book. Along the way, one thing became clear — there is no single formula for restaurant success, but there is an enormous amount to learn from the people who've found theirs.

"All of this content is focused on the people we serve, not on us. It's been a wonderful way to pour our energy into something we believe in and provide something meaningful to the industry."

There is no secret

After interviewing dozens of successful owners, ScheduleFly's Wil Brawley reached a counterintuitive conclusion: the secret to restaurant success is that there is no secret.

"Most books and articles about success tell us that if we just follow the steps, we will win. Yet every one of those authors misses a critical aspect: silent evidence. For every person who used grit to be successful, there may be ten others whose gritty efforts came up short."

That's why Restaurant Owners Uncorked doesn't preach a five-step plan. It lays out each owner's story — contradictions and all — and lets you find the pieces that fit your restaurant.

10 tips from Scott Maitland

Scott Maitland built Top of the Hill Restaurant & Brewery in Chapel Hill, N.C. over nearly 20 years. Here are a few of his sharpest lessons:

The business of the business

"There is the business of what you do, and there is the business of running your business. If you don't handle the business of your business well, that can kill you just as fast as if you don't handle the business of what you do."

The menu drives everything

"The menu really drives everything. It drives how you lay out your kitchen. It drives the staff you're going to have. It drives the customer segment you're going to bring in. If there is one manifestation of the restaurant, the menu is it."

The house always comes first

"If you can honestly look me in the eye and tell me you made a decision with the long-term best interests of the house in mind, then I will never get angry with you. Maybe I'll think you are wrong, and we can talk about it — but I will never be angry."

Owner stories that stuck

Richard Taubin — Friends Coastal Restaurant

Richard and his partner reopened a hurricane-battered Louisiana restaurant and roughly doubled its sales in four years. His philosophy? Employees come first, even before customers.

"If your employees are taken care of — compensated correctly, given the right tools, treated professionally — that shines on the customers."

Bret Oliverio — Sup Dogs

Bret built a college-town hot dog institution by refusing to be for everyone.

"My mentality, first and foremost, is to side with our restaurant and our staff. There is something to be said for sticking to your business and your staff and your beliefs."

Travis Todd — Ocean Odyssey

Travis's family has run a Maryland crab house since 1947. When imported crabmeat flooded the market, they drew a hard line.

"We would sooner take crab cakes off the menu. It's too much a core of what we are, what we came from, and what the history of our family business is."

Do it your own way

Running through every Restaurant Owners Uncorked story is the same thread: the owners who thrive are the ones who built the business their way, not the way convention — or well-meaning advice — prescribed.

"When somebody says 'You should,' followed by advice about your business, be wary. The only thing you should do is do it your way."
